Tech writing

Your App Isn't Broken. Your First Five Seconds Are.

Nobody downloads an app to admire it. They download it to fix a problem, and they give it about five seconds to prove it can.

In those five seconds, your users don't read your feature list. They look for one thing: can I do what I came to do, right now?

A slow splash screen says no. A sign-up wall says no. A button that does nothing on the first tap says no, loudly, and then they're gone, off to a competitor who said yes.

The fix isn't more features. It's fewer obstacles. Cut the screen nobody asked for. Test the first tap until it never fails. Make the first win arrive before the first doubt.

Great software doesn't impress people. It gets out of their way.

The Margin

I bought the book for the cover. Two hundred naira, a second-hand stall in Port Harcourt, rain tapping the tarpaulin like impatient fingers.

On page forty, someone had written in the margin: Don't believe him. He's lying here.

I read the paragraph again. The narrator, so sure of himself, so smooth, was lying. I had missed it entirely. Whoever held this pencil before me had seen straight through him.

After that, I read with a stranger at my shoulder. Page ninety: Here is where she stops trusting him. Page two hundred: I cried here. Don't tell.

On the last page, in the same careful hand: If you've reached this, you read it properly. Now pass it on.

I never found out who she was. But I keep a pencil in the book now, and I've left my own note on page one: Start here. Someone is already reading with you.
